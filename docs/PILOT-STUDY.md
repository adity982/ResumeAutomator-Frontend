# Five-to-ten participant pilot

## Invitation draft

I’m testing Resume Automator, a project-evidence workspace for job applications. Would you be willing to spend about 30 minutes trying it with one target job and two or three projects you are comfortable importing? I want to observe where the workflow is confusing, not judge your resume. Participation is voluntary; you can stop at any time. I will not publish your documents, name, screen recording or feedback without separate permission. Public repositories are enough; private access is optional and requires an enabled GitHub App.

## Consent script

Explain the task, time commitment and what will be recorded before starting. Ask permission separately for participation, written notes and any recording. Default to notes without recording. Explain that saved account content persists until deletion, that exports can contain selected source text, that GitHub disconnect does not remove imported excerpts, and that active-data deletion does not instantly erase provider backups. Do not promise a retention period until the operator has configured and disclosed it. Give the participant an operator contact and a participant code; keep identity mapping outside the shared tracker.

Confirm: “Do you consent to trying the task and letting me record de-identified task observations? You can skip any information, stop, or ask me to remove your research notes.” Do not bundle this with permission to publish. Store the consent date and chosen modes privately.

## Task and observation protocol

Recruit participants with multiple projects and a real job target. Use a staging environment verified for sign-in, isolation and deletion. Ask them to save the job, import selected projects, review contributions, choose a project set, inspect a citation, create/edit a draft, preview/approve/save a PDF, attach it, record Applied and recover after reload. Do not send an actual application as part of the study.

Time from the participant beginning the task to completing recovery. Record each intervention and the step that required it. If they stop, record a failure/abandonment, elapsed time and reason rather than excluding them. Never collect passwords, tokens or confidential employer files. Ask why a chosen project fits and whether any statement overstates their contribution.

Afterwards ask: What did you think the product would do? Which result did you disagree with? Did citations help you decide what to trust? What did you need to edit? What would bring you back for a second application? Collect the in-app feedback and ask whether they want their data removed.

## Measures defined before collection

- Unassisted completion: participants completing every required step without moderator intervention / all participants who began.
- Assisted completion: completed with one or more interventions / all participants who began.
- Time: report median and range among completers; report abandoned-task times separately.
- Source disagreement: selections the participant marked irrelevant or unsupported; report counts and reasons.
- Claim edits: number of drafted statements edited for accuracy; distinguish style edits.
- Repeat use: participants who voluntarily complete a second real target within seven days / participants eligible for the full observation window. No reminder-driven return should be labeled voluntary.

This sample is exploratory. Do not infer causality, interview uplift or population-level effectiveness. Report the participant denominator and missing data. The tracker starts empty because no study has been performed.

## Pilot decision

Investigate every cross-account leak, unsupported claim or lost-document event before inviting more users. Use unassisted completion and repeated confusion to choose the next UX changes. After the study, summarize actual counts, failures, changes and limitations, then ask for separate permission for any proposed public quote. Delete identifying notes within the configured/disclosed study retention period.
